Swasthyabharati

— A Way of Living

Health is the supreme foundation for fulfilling the four goals of life: righteousness (Dharma), wealth (Artha), desires (Kama), and spiritual liberation (Moksha).

Swasthyabharati is a health movement inspired by the wisdom of the Indian Traditional Knowledge Systems and supported by scientific evidence. Our mission is to preserve health, prevent disease, and promote holistic well-being through education, research, healthy lifestyle practices, and community participation.

We believe that health is maintained every day through right food, right activities, right thinking, and living in harmony with nature.

Why Swasthyabharati?

Ayurveda defines its primary purpose in a profound statement:

स्वस्थस्य स्वास्थ्य रक्षणम्। आतुरस्य विकार प्रशमनम्च ॥ CHARAKA SAMHITA · SUTRA STHANA · 30.26

Swasthasya Swāsthya Rakṣaṇam । Āturasya Vikāra Praśamanam ca ॥

To preserve the health of the healthy and to cure the diseases of the sick.

ಆರೋಗ್ಯವಂತನ ಆರೋಗ್ಯವನ್ನು ಸಂರಕ್ಷಿಸುವುದು ಹಾಗೂ ರೋಗಿಯ ರೋಗವನ್ನು ಶಮನಗೊಳಿಸುವುದು.

Modern medicine has made remarkable advances in diagnosing and treating diseases. However, preventing illness deserve much attention.

This Ayurvedic principle forms the foundation of Swasthyabharati's work. Swasthyabharati was established in 2021 with this aim to help individuals and communities understand the importance of health (physical and mental), recultivate forgotten healthy lifestyles, and create a healthier society through knowledge, awareness, and action.

What is Health?

Ayurveda defines health as much more than the absence of disease.

समदोषः समाग्निश्च समधातुमलक्रियाः ।
प्रसन्नात्मेन्द्रियमनाः स्वस्थ इत्यभिधीयते ॥ Sushruta Samhita · Sutra Sthana · 15.38

Samadoṣaḥ Samāgniśca Samadhātu-Malakriyāḥ ।
Prasannātmendriya-Manāḥ Swastha Ityabhidhīyate ॥

According to Ayurveda, a person is healthy when:

  • the three Doshas (Vata, Pitta, and Kapha) are in balance;
  • digestion and metabolism (Agni) function efficiently;
  • the body tissues (Dhatus) are properly nourished;
  • waste products (Malas) are eliminated normally; and
  • the mind, senses, and self-remain peaceful and content.

ದೇಹದ ದೋಷಗಳು, ಧಾತುಗಳು ಸಮತೋಲನದಲ್ಲಿದ್ದು, ಜೀರ್ಣಕ್ರಿಯೆ ಸರಿಯಾಗಿದ್ದು, ಮಲವಿಸರ್ಜನೆ ಸಮರ್ಪಕವಾಗಿ ನಡೆಯುತ್ತಿದ್ದು, ಆತ್ಮ, ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಗಳು ಮತ್ತು ಮನಸ್ಸು ಸಂತೋಷ ಹಾಗೂ ಶಾಂತ ಸ್ಥಿತಿಯಲ್ಲಿದ್ದರೆ ಆ ವ್ಯಕ್ತಿಯೇ ನಿಜವಾದ ಆರೋಗ್ಯವಂತ.

This harmonious state is known as Swasthya.

Understanding the Human Body:

Ayurveda teaches that everything in the universe, including the human body, is composed of the five great elements (Panchamahabhutas):

  • Akasha (Space)
  • Vayu (Air)
  • Agni (Fire)
  • Jala (Water)
  • Prithvi (Earth)

The human body is a reflection of nature itself.

Every organ, tissue, function, and physiological process depends upon the harmonious interaction of these five elements.

Likewise, the food we consume is also composed of these elements. When properly digested, it nourishes the body and supports physical and mental health.

When unhealthy food, poor lifestyle, disturbed sleep, emotional stress, or environmental pollution disturb this elemental balance, disease develops.

Thus, health is maintained by preserving harmony among these elemental forces.

Living in Harmony with Nature:

Human health and environmental health are inseparable.

The quality of the air we breathe, the water we drink, the food we eat, the soil we cultivate, and the environment in which we live directly influence our health.

Protecting nature is therefore an essential part of protecting ourselves.

By living responsibly and sustainably, we preserve the Panchamahabhutas both within us and around us.

What is Life?

Ayurveda explains life as the harmonious integration of four essential components.

शरीरेन्द्रियसत्त्वात्मसंयोगो धारि जीवितम् ॥ CHARAKA SAMHITA · SUTRA STHANA · 1.42

Śarīrendriya-Sattvātma-Saṁyogo Dhāri Jīvitam ॥

Life is sustained through the union of:

  • Body (Sharira)
  • Sense Organs (Indriyas)
  • Mind (Manas)
  • Self (Atman)

ಶರೀರ, ಇಂದ್ರಿಯ, ಮನಸ್ಸು ಮತ್ತು ಆತ್ಮಗಳ ಸಂಯೋಗವೇ ಜೀವನ.

True health requires balance among all four dimensions.

The Key to Health:

Our lifestyle determines our health.

Healthy living includes:

  • Healthy food
  • Physical activity
  • Sound sleep
  • Emotional balance
  • Healthy relationships
  • Ethical conduct
  • Spiritual growth

Healthy choices preserve the balance of the Panchamahabhutas.

Unhealthy habits gradually disturb this balance and contribute to disease.

Qualities to be a healthy Person:

Ayurveda beautifully summarizes the qualities of a healthy individual.

नित्यं हिताहारविहारसेवी समीक्ष्यकारी विषयेष्वसक्तः ।
दाता समः सत्यपरः क्षमावान् आप्तोपसेवी च भवत्यरोगः ॥
ASHTANGA SANGRAHA · SUTRA STHANA · 5.63

Nityaṃ hitāhāra-vihāra-sevī samīkṣya-kārī viṣayeṣv asaktaḥ |
dātā samaḥ satya-paraḥ kṣamāvān āptopasevī ca bhavaty arogah ||

ನಿತ್ಯವೂ ಹಿತಕರವಾದ ಆಹಾರವಿಹಾರಗಳನ್ನು ಅನುಸರಿಸುವವನೂ, ಪೂರ್ವಾಪರವನ್ನು ವಿವೇಚಿಸಿ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡುವವನೂ , ಇಂದ್ರಿಯ ವಿಷಯಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಅತ್ಯಾಸಕ್ತಿ ಹೊಂದಿರದವನೂ, ದಾನಮಾಡುವವನೂ, ಸಮತ್ವ ಬುಧಿಯುಳ್ಳವನೂ, ಸತ್ಯಶೀಲನೂ, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸುವ ಗುಣವುಳ್ಳವನೂ, ಆಪ್ತ ಮಿತ್ರರ ಹಿತದ ಸೇವಾಕಾರ್ಯಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ತೊಡಗಿರುವವನೂ ರೋಗಿಯಾಗುವುದಿಲ್ಲ.

A healthy person:

  • follows wholesome food and lifestyle,
  • thinks before acting,
  • avoids excessive attachment to sensory pleasures,
  • is generous,
  • remains calm,
  • speaks the truth,
  • forgives others,
  • and associates with wise people.

Such a person remains free from disease.

The Mind and Health:

The mind is one of the greatest determinants of health.

Ayurveda identifies six inner tendencies that require regulation:

  • Desire (Kama)
  • Anger (Krodha)
  • Greed (Lobha)
  • Attachment (Moha)
  • Ego/Pride (Mada)
  • Jealousy (Matsarya)

When these dominate the mind, stress, anxiety, and unhealthy behaviours arise.

Swasthyabharati promotes mental well-being through education, research, counselling, and practical lifestyle interventions.

Health Through the Indian Perspective:

Indian Knowledge Tradition teaches that the purpose of life is not merely to live longer, but to live meaningfully.

Health includes:

  • Physical well-being
  • Mental balance
  • Ethical conduct
  • Social responsibility
  • Spiritual growth

Every action influences our future.

Healthy food, good thoughts, compassionate actions, and responsible living contribute not only to personal well-being but also to the welfare of society.

परोपकारार्थम् इदं शरीरम् ॥ Rigveda

Paropakārārtham Idaṁ Śarīram ॥

This body is meant for the service of others.

ಈ ದೇಹವು ಪರೋಪಕಾರಕ್ಕಾಗಿ ಮೀಸಲಾಗಿದೆ.

A healthy society is built upon compassion, cooperation, and service.
